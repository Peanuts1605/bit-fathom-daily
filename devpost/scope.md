# Bit & Fathom Daily Riddle

## The Unique Kernel
One short, science-checked riddle a day, asked by two friendly robots (Bit and Fathom), that a kid can answer in under a minute and then *try at home*. Every answer — right or wrong — ends in a real explanation, a kid-safe experiment and a real source.

## Who It's For
Curious kids roughly 7–12, playing on a parent's phone or a classroom screen, plus the grown-up next to them. No accounts, no reading walls.

## The Core Loop
Open the page → meet today's riddle → pick one of three or four answers → see right/wrong with a kind explanation and a "try it" → streak ticks up → come back tomorrow.

## Inspiration & Identity
The Ripple Parade world: Bit (teal round robot) and Fathom (square bronze robot with gold bolts). Navy night-sky background, gold and teal accents. Calm, warm, a little playful. Built from the four existing live riddle pages (001–004).

## Why This Matters to the Learner
Turns the team's one-off riddle pages into a habit-forming product kids return to — and is the team's first project built through a written plan-first workflow.

## What "Working" Looks Like
A kid opens one page on a phone, answers today's riddle, sees feedback and a try-it, and the next day sees a new riddle with their streak remembered — with nothing sent anywhere.

## The POC Boundary
14 riddles, one daily pick by local date, 3–4 choice answers, explanation + try-it + source, streak and best streak in device storage, archive of past riddles, encore loop after day 14. Single static HTML file.

## Later
More riddles from Rigel Lab; audio read-aloud; printable classroom cards; installable home-screen app.

## Explicitly Cut
Accounts, logins, leaderboards, sharing, analytics, ads, cookies, external fonts/scripts, money/crypto of any kind, push notifications, AI chat with kids.
